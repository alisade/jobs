Forward Deployed Engineer (EE / Edge AI lean) - Study Plan

6-week plan plus an optional add-on week. Weekdays ~2-3 h, weekends one long block for the workbook exercises. Checkboxes save in this browser.

Overview

The role: a forward deployed engineer who embeds on site with strategic clients and their Edge AI teams, frames the problem, owns the architecture, and writes the production code: Python data pipelines, API integrations and MLOps/AI workflows, with an Edge AI, signal-processing and LLM/agent lean. The posting puts "EE background preferred" in the title, asks for strong Python and 10+ years of production software, and expects travel and embedding with clients.

Honest fit. The customer-facing half is your strongest card: embedded delivery in locked-down networks, finding the real cause, leaving assets the client owns. The technical half is mostly new: production Python as the main language, MLOps, Edge AI deployment and signal processing. You have no EE degree or signal-processing work on record; do not imply one. Ask the recruiter first whether EE is a hard screen and what the Edge AI teams deploy on which hardware, and decide your travel limit before the first call. Close the gap with a portfolio that shows measured results on real sensor data, and say plainly that the Python, MLOps and Edge AI work is recent and portfolio-based.

Why 6 weeks plus an optional add-on

  • The highlights plan has six focused weeks, one per gap, ordered by what the posting requires: Python and pipelines first (required), then signals (the title's filter), MLOps (required), Edge AI and fleet (the Edge AI teams), and LLMs at the edge plus the FDE write-up last.
  • The shared core is woven in, not repeated: Claude Code as your harness in week 1, Pydantic contracts in week 1, evals as promotion gates in week 3, agent patterns, tool calling and LLM security in week 6 and on the AI page.
  • Weekdays are about 2-3 h of reading and small builds; the weekend block is for the 16 workbook exercises (1-3 h each). About 15-20 h per week.
  • Week 7 is optional: API integrations with a CMMS and a streaming DSP drill.
  • If time is short: weeks 1, 2 and 6 plus the Interview prep tab cover the required Python, the EE filter and the FDE story in three weeks.

Portfolio project: an edge-to-cloud machine-health kit, written up as an FDE engagement

Frame it as a client engagement from the first commit. The README opens with a one-page brief: "a plant operator wants early warning of bearing failure on 10 machines, with a site network that blocks most outbound traffic", the baseline (time-based maintenance, no early warning) and the targets.

  • Data: CWRU bearing or MIMII machine sounds replayed as live sensor streams over MQTT or Kafka, plus a simulator for injecting new faults.
  • Pipeline (Python): schema validation, windowing, FFT features with SciPy, idempotent writes keyed by device and window, a backfill command, data quality checks.
  • Model: a small anomaly detector tracked in MLflow, exported to ONNX and quantized to INT8, promoted by a CI gate on recall and false-alarm rate against the champion.
  • Edge: ONNX Runtime on a Raspberry Pi, a Jetson or an ARM64 container; features and alerts go up, not raw audio; a SQLite buffer replays without duplicates.
  • Fleet: OTA to a small simulated fleet: canary one device, promote or roll back automatically; every device reports its model version.
  • Agent: a read-only operator assistant on a local model with an optional cloud model by policy; it cannot change device or model state.
  • Locked-down mode: installs from a wheelhouse and saved images, through a proxy with a custom CA bundle, with no other internet.
  • Measured outcome: recall and false alarms per machine-week, lead time, FP32 vs INT8 size and latency, bandwidth saved, fleet rollout time.
  • Customer-owned handoff: model card, runbooks (retrain, roll back, add a device), architecture diagram, an eval suite the client can rerun, a "what we would do next" note.

One-line pitch: "I have spent 26 years embedded with client teams inside locked-down enterprise networks, finding the real cause and leaving them with systems they own; I now build the edge-to-cloud ML and agent pipelines that sit on top, in Python, with measured results."

Week 2 - Signal processing and sensor data

The 'EE background preferred' filter. You will not claim an EE degree; you will be able to talk about sample rates, spectra and filters without hand-waving, and show tested code that works on real bearing data. Everything here is done in NumPy and SciPy, with synthetic signals of known content first, then CWRU or MIMII.

MonSampling, Nyquist and aliasing; the FFT

Sample rate, Nyquist frequency (fs/2), aliasing and why anti-aliasing happens before sampling or before decimation. The DFT and FFT: bins, resolution fs/N, real FFT, magnitude and phase.

Tasks

  • Generate a 120 Hz sine plus noise at 1 kHz; find the peak with np.fft.rfft; then sample at 200 Hz and show the alias at 80 Hz
  • Compute the bin spacing for N = 4096 at 20 kHz and for a 2-second window; write down which you would use for 1 Hz separation
  • Read Think DSP chapters 1-2 (or the DSP guide chapter on sampling) and redo one exercise in NumPy
  • Write a unit test that feeds a tone of known frequency and asserts the detected peak within half a bin

Materials

  • Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem (Wikipedia)
  • NumPy FFT routines
  • SciPy FFT tutorial
  • Think DSP (free book, Python)
  • Think DSP code and notebooks
  • The Scientist and Engineer's Guide to Digital Signal Processing (free book)

How this applies to this role

Forward Deployed Engineer (Edge AI): the seven areas apply to an operator assistant on a small local model at the plant. Tool design (1) is three read-only tools over alerts, features and the model card; the loop (2) runs on llama.cpp or Ollama with a cloud fallback only by policy; context (3) is short, because small models have small windows; security (4) is the one that bites: alert notes are untrusted content and the dispatcher, not the prompt, keeps the assistant read-only; evals (5) run on the real local model with latency per answer; RAG (6) is optional (manuals, past work orders); observability (7) is one span per model and tool call. Week 6 covers the edge specifics.

SignalsCan you work with our sensor data?

The EE filter. Be honest about the degree and concrete about the skills.

Rehearse

  • "What sample rate would you use for bearing monitoring on a 1,800 rpm pump?"
  • "Why does a fault frequency show up in the envelope spectrum but not the raw spectrum?"
  • "You see a peak at exactly 702 Hz on four new machines. What do you suspect?"

Talk track

  • Say plainly: no EE degree, but sampling, FFT, filtering and feature extraction in NumPy/SciPy on CWRU data with tests.
  • Resonances sit in the kHz range: sample at 20-25 kHz, low-pass before any decimation, window and use Welch for summaries.
  • Impacts ring a resonance; the defect rate modulates it; demodulation turns sidebands into a line at BPFO.
  • Identical frequency across machines points at the pipeline: aliasing of a gear mesh through decimation without a filter (w2-t1).
