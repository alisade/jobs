Senior Staff Platform Engineer - GCP, Terraform, GKE fleets, Crossplane

6-week plan plus an optional add-on week for a remote Senior Staff Platform Engineer / SME role (GCP or Alibaba, Terraform and IaC, migration into Terraform). Weekdays ~2-3 h, weekends one long block for the workbook exercises. Checkboxes save in this browser.

Overview

The role is a Senior Staff Platform Engineer and subject matter expert for infrastructure security management and core cloud services (the posting names MDM, RIQ and RDM). You would be the first line of architectural authority for reliability and DevSecOps problems, own IaC with Crossplane, Terraform and Helm ("a must-have"), design zero-downtime maintenance for fleets of Kubernetes clusters, mentor platform engineers, remove toil with AI tools and agentic workflows, and take a 12-hour on-call rotation. The cloud is GCP or Alibaba, and the header asks for "migration experience from cloud to Terraform". This plan does not teach Kubernetes, Terraform or CI/CD: you already run them. It goes deep and fast on the GCP brand of those skills, Crossplane, Prometheus-native observability, Go, and an agent workflow that keeps its approval gates.

Honest fit note. This is the closest fit of all your target roles: the discipline is what you have done for 26 years. The hard filter is the cloud brand. The header says "Need GCP or Alibaba" and "We do not need AWS or Azure experience", so a keyword screen may reject an AWS/Azure resume. You cannot fix that with wording; you fix it with GCP work you can point at (the public portfolio repo below) and a Google Cloud certification booked in week 1. The posting also contradicts itself twice (see Interview prep): ask the recruiter early whether GCP production time is a hard requirement, and whether the platform runs on GCP or Alibaba.

TueIAM, service accounts and impersonation

No inline policy document per role: you bind roles (basic, predefined, custom) to principals on a resource, and the bindings inherit. Deny policies and IAM Conditions are the extra levers. Service accounts are attached to workloads and impersonated; keys are the thing org policy forbids.

Tasks

  • Read the IAM overview; write one paragraph contrasting an AWS role trust policy plus permission policy with a GCP allow policy binding (who, what role, on which resource)
  • Read service accounts overview and best practices; list the five practices you would enforce (no keys, one SA per workload, no basic roles, impersonation via roles/iam.serviceAccountTokenCreator, restrict the default compute SA)

  • Find two predefined roles that are too broad for a CI deployer and write a custom role or a condition-scoped binding instead

Week 2 - GKE fleet operations and zero-downtime upgrades

"Design zero downtime maintenance strategies for fleets of kubernetes clusters" is the most concrete technical duty in the posting. Kubernetes transfers from your EKS work; the GKE knobs do not: release channels, maintenance windows and exclusion scopes, surge versus blue-green node pool upgrades, upgrade quota, cluster notifications and fleets. Skip the Kubernetes basics; go straight to how GKE decides when and how your nodes are replaced.

MonHow GKE upgrades: control plane, nodes, release channels, skew

GKE upgrades the control plane automatically; node pools follow according to your settings. Channels are Rapid, Regular, Stable and Extended ("no channel" is deprecated). Pick a channel per environment so dev sees a version before prod.

Week 4 - Crossplane v2, Helm and ArgoCD at fleet scale

Crossplane is the first tool in the must-have IaC line and the one you have never run, so it gets three days. Learn v2: namespaced XRs and namespaced managed resources, compositions as a pipeline of functions, claims as a legacy v1 feature you will still meet in existing installs. Helm and ArgoCD are strengths; the work there is presenting them as fleet-scale design: library charts, ApplicationSets and a promotion path that cannot hit every cluster at once.

MonThe Crossplane model and what changed in v2

Managed resources (MRs) map to cloud APIs; providers install them; an XRD defines a composite resource (XR); a composition turns an XR into MRs. In v2, XRs and MRs can be namespaced, claims are legacy, and compositions run functions such as function-patch-and-transform.
