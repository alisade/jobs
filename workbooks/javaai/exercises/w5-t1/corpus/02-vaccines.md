# Vaccines and immunity

## Innate and adaptive immunity

Innate immunity responds within hours using barriers, phagocytes and inflammation, and it does not improve with repeated exposure. Adaptive immunity uses B lymphocytes and T lymphocytes that recognise specific antigens and improve after exposure.

## How vaccines work

A vaccine presents an antigen, or instructions to make one, without causing the disease. The adaptive immune system produces antibodies and long lived memory cells, so a later infection triggers a faster and stronger secondary response.

## Types of vaccines

Live attenuated vaccines use a weakened organism, inactivated vaccines use a killed one, subunit vaccines use purified proteins, and mRNA vaccines deliver messenger RNA that cells translate into the antigen protein. Adjuvants are added to some vaccines to strengthen the response.

## Herd immunity

Herd immunity protects people who cannot be vaccinated when enough of the population is immune. The threshold depends on R0: the higher the reproduction number, the larger the immune fraction needed to stop sustained spread, roughly one minus one over R0.
