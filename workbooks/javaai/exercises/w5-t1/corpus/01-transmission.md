# Pathogen transmission

## Routes of transmission

Pathogens move between hosts by several routes. Respiratory droplets travel short distances when an infected person coughs or sneezes. Airborne aerosols are smaller and can stay suspended in a room for hours. Direct contact transmission needs touching an infected person, and indirect contact goes through contaminated surfaces called fomites.

## Vectors and reservoirs

A vector is a living carrier, usually an arthropod such as a mosquito or tick, that moves a pathogen from one host to another. A reservoir is the habitat where the pathogen normally lives and multiplies, for example bats for some coronaviruses or rodents for hantavirus.

## The basic reproduction number

The basic reproduction number R0 is the average number of secondary infections produced by one infectious case in a fully susceptible population. When R0 is above one an outbreak can grow; when the effective reproduction number falls below one it declines.

## Breaking the chain

Public health measures target links in the chain of infection: hand hygiene and disinfection remove fomites, ventilation and masks reduce aerosols, vector control drains standing water, and isolation shortens the infectious period that a case spends among susceptible contacts.
