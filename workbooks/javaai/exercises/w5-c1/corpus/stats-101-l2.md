# Sampling and inference

## Samples and populations

A population is the whole group of interest and a sample is the subset you measure. A statistic describes a sample and a parameter describes the population. Random sampling gives every member a known chance of selection and reduces selection bias.

## Sampling distributions

Repeated samples give different statistics; the distribution of a statistic across samples is its sampling distribution. The central limit theorem says the sampling distribution of the mean is approximately normal for large samples, whatever the population shape.

## Confidence intervals

A ninety five percent confidence interval is built by a method that captures the true parameter in ninety five percent of repeated samples. Larger samples give narrower intervals because the standard error shrinks with the square root of the sample size.

## Hypothesis tests

A hypothesis test compares data with a null hypothesis. The p value is the probability of results at least this extreme if the null were true. A small p value is evidence against the null, not the probability that the null is true, and statistical significance is not practical importance.
