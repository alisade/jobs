# platform/w5-t1 - The RIQ burn-rate alert never fired

**Type:** troubleshooting | **Estimate:** 1.5-2 hours | **Study plan:** Week 5 (Prometheus, Managed Service for Prometheus, SLO burn-rate alerting)

## Scenario

On 2026-11-04 from 13:12 to 13:50 UTC riq-api on prod-blue returned 503 for about 6% of requests.
That is a 60x burn of the 99.9% availability SLO, and the multi-window fast-burn alert (1h and 5m
above 14.4x) should have paged by about 13:30. PagerDuty shows nothing; Support found the outage from
customer tickets. Read `artifacts/PAGE.txt` first: it is the platform lead's request for the PIR.

You do not get a live cluster. You get the evidence below and a TSDB extract of what Managed Service
for Prometheus scraped that day, plus a small offline evaluator so you can replay the rules exactly.

## The PromQL subset

`tools/promql_mini.py` supports: selectors with `=`, `!=`, `=~`, `!~` (a missing label equals `""`;
5 minute lookback); `rate()` and `increase()` over a range (first and last sample in the window,
counter resets handled, no extrapolation); `absent()`, `vector()`; `sum`, `max`, `min`, `avg`,
`count` with `by (...)` or `without (...)`; `+ - * /` between scalars and vectors with one-to-one
matching on the full label set (or `on(...)` / `ignoring(...)`); comparison filters; `and`, `or`,
`unless`. No subqueries, `offset`, `bool`, `group_left`. Rules are evaluated every 60 s in file order;
recording rule results are written back and can be selected by later rules; `for:` is honoured.

## Your task

1. Replay: `make alerts`. Then explore with `make query Q='...' AT=2026-11-04T13:30:00Z`, for example the
   raw series, each recording rule and each alert expression on its own.
2. Find the root cause. Check, with evidence, the two theories from the incident channel: the canary
   silence and the Alertmanager route change.
3. Fix `rules/riq-slo.rules.yaml` so the alerts work with the labels managed collection attaches.
   Decide whether the ratio is per cluster or across the fleet, and write down why.
4. Add a guard: an alert that fires when the series the SLO alerts depend on disappear, so this class
   of failure cannot be silent again. Give it a severity that does not wake someone at 3 AM unless you
   argue otherwise.
5. Fill in `ANSWER.md`.

## Acceptance criteria (what the grader checks)

- Replaying the grader's own scenarios with your rule file: the fast-burn alert fires within 20 minutes
  of 13:12 on the incident data with `severity: page`, `team: riq`; nothing fires on a healthy day; a
  4-minute 20% spike does not page; a sustained burn rate of 12 pages through the slow alert only; a
  sustained burn rate of 16 pages fast; an outage on prod-green carrying half the traffic pages within
  40 minutes; when the riq-api series stop matching (label drift), a guard alert fires within 30
  minutes and the SLO alerts do not.
- The rules no longer select or group on `service`.
- `ANSWER.md`: the root cause with evidence (query output), the red herrings ruled out, the fix and
  production follow-ups.
